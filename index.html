Who can share a loud space?

01 · Map objective

Noise decides who can use a space, and when

The void decks, playgrounds, fitness corners and hawker tables of Teck Ghee are shared by every age group, but rarely at the same time. Noise is one reason. A playground that suits children after school can drive away a senior resting nearby, and traffic from Ang Mo Kio Avenue 10 and the CTE can empty a space for everyone.

This project asks residents to measure noise in the public spaces they use, with a free phone app, and to note who else is there. Over a few weeks the map shows which spaces are loud, when, from what, and which age groups share them at those moments. Residents can bring this evidence to the Town Council and NEA, and it informs an NUS study on designing spaces where generations can be together.

03 · Data gathering and management

How a reading travels

Measure30 seconds in a public space, noting who is there
ReportGoogle Form, on this site or by QR code at void decks and playgrounds
StoreResponses collect in a Google Sheet
CheckWeekly review by a student moderator
MapPostal code placed with OneMap, shown on the map

Residents submit readings through a Google Form, on this website or by QR codes at void decks and playgrounds. Each report gives the nearest block's postal code, the type of space, a decibel reading averaged over 30 seconds, the source, the time, the age groups present and how the noise affects the respondent's use of the space. No names or unit numbers are collected, and ages are recorded only in broad groups.

The map reads the linked Google Sheet directly and places each postal code with Mapbox. A student moderator reviews it weekly, removing readings outside 30 to 130 dB and flagging outliers. Uncalibrated phone meters mean the map compares places and times, not exact levels. A monthly summary goes to the Residents' Network and Town Council.
